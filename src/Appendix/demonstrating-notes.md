# Intermission: Notes on demonstratives

*Intermissions are optional.*

The "like so" determiners are parallel to よう, but よう isn't part of a demonstrative category.
It means just some particular "kind".

<pre>
こんな人、今まで見たことがない。
I've never seen anyone like this before.

そんな人がいるなんて思わなかった。
I never thought someone like that existed.

あんな人、見たことない。
I've never seen anyone like that.

嵐のような人だった。
They were like a storm.
</pre>

There's an irregular demonstrative determiner, かの.
かの is used for things that are known to the first and second person,
but are distant in a very abstract way.
かの is essentially unused outside of very formal or literary uses.

<pre>
かの地へ向かうための準備を整えた。
Preparations were made to head for that land. (We prepared to travel to that land.)
</pre>

Also, the pronoun for "he", かれ, used to be かの's demonstrative pronoun.
かの is very literary and you're only likely to encounter it used for literary effect.
