# Intermission: Long vowels and gemination

*Intermissions are optional.*

In Japanese, "long vowel" doesn't mean what it means when you're talking about English.
Japanese long vowels literally last longer.

Compare the short vowel in おじさん with the long vowel in おじいさん:

<pre>
おじさんが来たよ
The man is here.

そこにおじいさんがいた。
There was an old man there.
</pre>

Japanese also has something called "gemination".
Gemination means that the speaker briefly stops on a consonant.
In Japanese, this adds the same amount of time as using a long vowel.
Practice the following to get a hang of the idea:

|       |         |                 |
| ---   | ---     | ---             |
| Kata  | Ka-ta   | かた            |
| Kaata | Ka-a-ta | かあた (かーた) |
| Katta | Ka-t-ta | かった          |

The letter t isn't pronounced twice in the third example.
There's a point in time when the mouth stops making the first "a" sound and is in the position of
the "t" sound, but the air is blocked.
It's not released until the "ta".

The きた of 来た has a short consonant.
The きった of 切った stops on the doubled consonant.

<pre>
彼が来たようだ。
It seems he came.

髪を切ったのか？
Did you cut your hair?
</pre>

Long e is sometimes spelled as ei (えい).
Not all instances of えい are a long `e`.
But all instances of えい that are a long `e` can also be pronounced as literally え followed by い.
It can sound stiff, but it's still correct.
To illustrate:

<pre>
えい (not a long e): Only pronounced as えい
えい (is a long e): Can be pronounced えい or ええ
</pre>

The えい in 映画 (えいが) commonly represents a long e.
In 絵入り (えいり), the え of 絵 and the い of 入り belong to separate parts of the compound.

<pre>
何の映画を見たんだ？
What movie did you watch?

絵入り新聞は大々的にこの事件について報じていた。
The illustrated newspaper reported extensively on this incident.
</pre>

Long `o` is sometimes spelled as `ou` (おう).
Not all instances of おう are a long `o`.
Unlike えい, sequences of おう can only be pronounced a certain way,
depending on whether it's a long `o` or not.
おう is used this way because it reduces how many words have the same spelling.

<pre>
おう (not a long o): Only pronounced おう
おう (is a long o): Only pronounced おお
</pre>

王様 (おうさま) illustrates a long o.
The verb 追う (おう) has a separate う.

<pre>
あー、王様が来たっぽいね。
Oh, it looks like the king's here.

彼を追う必要はありません。
There's no need to chase him.
</pre>

お and う are so close that the distinction between おう and おお
might be hard to hear or articulate in casual speech,
but the distinction is always *intended*.

Most instances of おう that are not long o sounds are verb endings.
But there's one verb ending where this is not the case: the volitional, taught later.

<pre>
じゃあ、一緒に行こう。
Okay, let's go together.
</pre>
