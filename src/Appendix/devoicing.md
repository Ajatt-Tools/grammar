# Intermission: Devoicing

*Intermissions are optional.*

In certain places, the vowels "u" and "i" can be "devoiced",
meaning that they're not pronounced at all.
です and でした are examples.
It's still acceptable to pronounce the vowels in these cases, but you'll sound weird.

|         |          |        |
| ---     | ---      | ---    |
| Desu    | De-s     | です   |
| Deshita | De-sh-ta | でした |

<pre>
今日はいい天気です。
It's nice weather today.

私は大学生でした。
I was a university student.
</pre>

These sentences show the written forms.
Devoicing is something you hear, not see.

Don't bother learning any more about devoicing until you're much better at Japanese.
It'll make it harder to notice irregular devoicing patterns that people don't bother teaching.
