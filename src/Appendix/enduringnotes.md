# Intermission: Notes on ている and てある

*Intermissions are optional.*

Progressive ている can also describe something that happens regularly,
not just something happening right now.

<pre>
学校に通っている。
I go to school.
</pre>

を with てある is the first of many nonintuitive constructions
that really mess with western ideas of subject, object, and so on.
No grammar explanation will help more than a little immersion.
[Immerse](https://ajatt.top/blog/mass-immersion.html), watch more anime, read, and read more.

<pre>
部屋を用意してある。
I've prepared a room.

食堂に飲み物が用意してある。
Drinks have been prepared in the dining hall.
</pre>

The closest thing to てある for い-adjectives is くある,
which is only used in constructions that try to treat the adjective like a verb, as in 悪くあります.
On its own, though, it is wrong or unnatural, like 悪くある.
A couple adverb-based constructions,
like 多くある,
overlap with this くある, but they are different.
You will definitely run into forms like 悪くあります,
but this is a way of making polite verbal statements with adjectives,
not expressing an enduring state.

<pre>
体調は悪くありません。
My health is fine.

やることは多くある。
There are many things to do.
</pre>
