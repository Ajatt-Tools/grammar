For a piece of trivia, ば comes from the topic marker, は.
The h- syllables used to be pronounced with a "p", which is why their voiced versions use a "b".
The topic marker is an edge case where a "p" evolved into a "w" instead of an "h".

<pre>
雨が降れ<b>ば</b>、足音<b>は</b>ほとんど聞こえない。
If it rains, footsteps are almost inaudible.
</pre>

This shows how ば and は are used now, not how they developed historically.
