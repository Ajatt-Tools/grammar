# Intermission: Notes on the passive

*Intermissions are useful, but if they stress you out, you shouldn't read them.*

Sometimes から is used for the agent of a passive verb.
This shows a difference in nuance.
から is sometimes unacceptable.

<pre>
そして、二人して母<b>から</b>怒られたものだ。
And the two of us used to get scolded by our mother.
</pre>

Keigo loves passives.
You're on your own here.

<pre>
先生は帰られた。
The teacher went home. (respectful)
</pre>

Adjectives are inherently intransitive and stateful.
This even applies to ones that *look* logically transitive, like 好き.

<pre>
私は猫<b>が</b>好きだ。
I like cats.
</pre>

English has a couple transitive pairs, like raise/rise and lay/lie,
but modern spoken English is trying to merge these together,
since modern spoken English doesn't care that much about transitivity anymore
(I blew up the car vs The car blew up).
This merge is not happening in Japanese.

<pre>
俺は部屋のドア<b>を開けた</b>。
I opened the door to the room.

その時、部屋のドア<b>が開いた</b>。
At that moment, the door to the room opened.
</pre>

In the first sentence, the door takes を. In the second, it takes が.
For Japanese transitive pairs, see [Lesson 24](../Section1/Part2/Lesson24.md).
