# Section 2

At this point, you should have been
[immersing](https://ajatt.top/blog/whats-immersion.html)
[all the time](https://ajatt.top/blog/whats-ajatt.html).
Skim the later lessons to see what the
guide has to say about any unfamiliar grammar you run into.
The rest of this guide is part advice, part reference.

Language learning takes time, even if you put in a lot of effort.
You have to apply yourself,
but you won't see big results until you've done it long enough.
Think about what that means.
The very first time you start reading,
everything feels really weird,
and you're not going to "get it".
That's okay.
It would be weird if you didn't react that way.
But [input](https://ajatt.top/blog/whats-immersion.html) (listening and reading)
is the only way to make that weird feeling go away.

And that's true for every little part of immersion.
New words, grammar patterns, ways Japanese people communicate, and figures of speech.
They all feel weird and foreign at first, and they don't make sense.
But eventually, once you've been exposed to them enough,
you'll kind of start to "get it."
That's why you've got to immerse.
You have to start the process of getting your brain familiar with it
in ways we still don't consciously understand yet.

This guide can't teach you Japanese.
Nobody can teach you Japanese.
The only way to learn Japanese is to
[understand messages](https://ajatt.top/blog/introduction-to-learning-japanese.html#the-right-mindset)
that are written or spoken in Japanese.
But why focus on "understanding messages" in particular?

Two reasons:

- If something is way out of your depth, you won't learn anything from it.
  If you don't understand what's being said,
  you won't learn anything new about the vocabulary and grammar used in it.
- It's totally possible to consume something and understand its individual parts
  but have no idea what it "means".
  You can break 食べてきました down into its individual parts,
  and that helps you see the syntax,
  but unless you understand **what** it's saying, not just **how** it's said,
  you won't acquire anything from the message.
  Understanding the content or meaning of the message itself is a prerequisite for learning from it.

A lot of people start learning a language and focus on picking apart messages
one word or phrase at a time, like it's a puzzle.
This can make things seem less confusing,
but if you keep doing it all the time,
you'll miss the big picture and fail to understand a lot of messages.

This gets worse when you think your grammar guide teaches you everything you need to know.
In reality, guides can't teach you all the high level stuff
unless you're some kind of linguistics savant.

Once you figure out,
"Huh, 食べてきました seems to be used in \<situation X\> a lot",
that's when it's time to break it down and try to see what the individual parts contribute,
comparing them to similar phrases you already know.
If you always start with the individual parts,
you'll miss the general idea
and fail to associate the phrase with the situation.

That association is what lets your brain acquire the language
and get a fluent handle on it.
Noticing what things mean when they're used is what you need to do if you want to learn Japanese.
No textbook, not even the best one, and no grammar guide, not even this one, can give you that.
It's all up to you.

Don't think. Feel.
